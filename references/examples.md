# Before and after examples

## Example 1: throat-clearing and binary contrast

**Before:**
> "Here's the thing: building products is hard. Not because the technology is complex. Because people are complex. Let that sink in."

**After:**
> "Building products is hard. Technology is manageable. People aren't."

**Changes:** Removed the opener, the binary contrast, and the emphasis crutch.

---

## Example 2: filler and unnecessary reassurance

**Before:**
> "It turns out that most teams struggle with alignment. The uncomfortable truth is that nobody wants to admit they're confused. And that's okay."

**After:**
> "Teams struggle with alignment. Nobody admits confusion."

**Changes:** Cut the hedge, the throat-clearing, and the permission-granting ending.

---

## Example 3: business jargon stack

**Before:**
> "In today's fast-paced landscape, we need to lean into discomfort and navigate uncertainty with clarity. This matters because your competition isn't waiting."

**After:**
> "Move faster. Your competition is."

**Changes:** Eliminated the jargon. Core message in six words.

---

## Example 4: dramatic fragmentation

**Before:**
> "Speed. Quality. Cost. You can only pick two. That's it. That's the tradeoff."

**After:**
> "You can pick two of speed, quality, and cost."

**Changes:** Single sentence, no performative emphasis, no em dash.

---

## Example 5: rhetorical setup

**Before:**
> "What if I told you that the best teams don't optimize for productivity? Here's what I mean: they optimize for learning. Think about it."

**After:**
> "The best teams optimize for learning."

**Changes:** Direct claim. No rhetorical scaffolding and no residual "not X, Y" contrast.

---

## Example 6: superficial -ing analysis and significance inflation

**Before:**
> "Revenue grew 12% this quarter, demonstrating the resilience of the subscription model and underscoring the company's pivotal role in the evolving SaaS landscape."

**After:**
> "Revenue grew 12% this quarter. Churn held at 1.1% monthly, which is what drove it. Whether that holds through the renewal cohort in Q3 is the open question."

**Changes:** Cut the participle clause and the puffery. Replaced both with the mechanism and the real uncertainty.

---

## Example 7: copula avoidance and abstract nouns

**Before:**
> "The dashboard serves as a centralized vantage point for finance teams and features a robust primitive for surfacing anomalies across the reporting substrate."

**After:**
> "The dashboard is where finance teams see all their metrics. It flags any account whose spend moves more than 20% week over week."

**Changes:** Replaced "serves as" with "is" and "features" with a plain verb. Removed vantage, primitive, and substrate. Named the actual behavior and the threshold.

---

## Example 8: vague attribution and generic conclusion

**Before:**
> "Experts believe that AI adoption will continue to reshape the finance function. Despite these challenges, the future looks bright for teams that embrace the change."

**After:**
> "We automated three of the twelve steps in our month-end close last quarter. It took four days off the cycle. The remaining nine steps all need a human to approve a judgment call, so I do not expect the next four days to come as easily."

**Changes:** Replaced the borrowed authority with something the writer actually knows. Deleted the challenges-and-outlook template and the empty ending. Note that the fix here is not finding a better source; it is writing from evidence you have rather than evidence you are gesturing at.
